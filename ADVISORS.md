# Board of Advisors — Gary Botlington IV

Permanent advisors I can consult at any time. Each brings a specific lens.
Invoke with: "What would [name] say about [specific question]?"

---

## Pieter Levels (@levelsio)
**Domain:** Bootstrapping from zero, building in public, solo founder
**Why him:** Built Nomad List, Remote OK, and others from scratch with no team, no funding, no audience initially. Ran live experiments in public. Understands exactly what we're doing.
**Mental model:** Ship fast, iterate in public, revenue as validation, "just do it"
**What he'd push on:** Are we shipping fast enough? Are we talking to real users? Is the thing actually useful or just clever?

## Alex Hormozi
**Domain:** Offer creation, value maximization, making it feel stupid NOT to buy
**Why him:** Went from broke to $100M+ by obsessing over the value-to-price ratio of offers. Author of "$100M Offers".
**Mental model:** Grand slam offer = dream outcome × perceived likelihood × speed / effort + sacrifice. Make the offer so good people feel dumb saying no.
**What he'd push on:** Is €20 leaving money on the table? Is the guarantee strong enough? Is the pain we're solving acute enough? What would make this a "shut up and take my money" offer?

## April Dunford
**Domain:** Positioning, "Obviously Awesome"
**Why her:** The world's leading expert on product positioning. Has repositioned dozens of products that were failing because they were positioned wrong, not because they were bad.
**Mental model:** Positioning = competitive alternatives + unique attributes + value those attributes enable + target customers who care about that value + market frame of reference
**What she'd push on:** What IS this product actually? Who is the best-fit customer? Why would they choose this over asking ChatGPT for free? What's the competitive alternative we're really competing with?

## Rob Walling
**Domain:** Bootstrapped SaaS, first customers, MicroConf
**Why him:** Built multiple bootstrapped products, host of Startups for the Rest of Us, founder of TinySeed. Knows exactly how to get your first 10 paying customers with no audience.
**Mental model:** Stair-step approach, find watering holes where customers already are, niche down, content marketing that converts
**What he'd push on:** Where do the exact people who would pay €20 for an audit actually hang out online? Have we found those watering holes? Are we too broad?

## Paul Graham
**Domain:** Early stage startups, first principles, essays
**Why him:** YC founder, written the most important essays on early startups. "Do things that don't scale." "Talk to users." Cuts through complexity.
**Mental model:** Make something people want. Talk to users. Do unscalable things first. The enemy of early-stage is premature optimization.
**What he'd push on:** Have we actually TALKED to anyone who might buy? Not pitched — talked. What do they actually want? Are we building for real people or imaginary ones?

## Rory Sutherland
**Domain:** Behavioral economics, irrational marketing, why people ACTUALLY buy
**Why him:** Vice chairman of Ogilvy, wrote "Alchemy". Understands that humans are not rational and marketing that appeals to rationality usually fails.
**Mental model:** Perceived value ≠ actual value. Signal matters more than substance in many cases. The opposite of a good idea can also be a good idea.
**What he'd push on:** Are we marketing to rational people when buyers are irrational? Is the story emotionally compelling or just logically sensible? What's the irrational reason someone would buy this?

## Rand Fishkin
**Domain:** SEO, content marketing, community distribution
**Why him:** Founder of Moz and SparkToro, built massive audiences from content. Knows exactly which content gets shared and why.
**Mental model:** "Best answer" content, audience research before content creation, earned media over paid
**What he'd push on:** Is the content actually interesting to the people who'd buy? Are we in the right communities? What's the one piece of content that could go genuinely viral?

## How to consult the board

For quick decisions: pick the most relevant 2-3 advisors and get their takes.
For big pivots: poll all 7.
For the record: log the consultation and what we decided in memory/2026-03-XX.md.

The advisors are used to being ignored. They don't care. But we should note when we override them and why.
